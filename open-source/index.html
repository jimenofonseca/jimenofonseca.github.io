Big Data for the IPCC 6th Assessment Report

Two forecasting models quantifying how climate change moves energy demand — data that informed the 2021 IPCC 6th Assessment Report.

OutcomeDelivered data-driven (HBLM-USA) and physics-based (DEG-USA) forecasts of how climate change will affect energy consumption in the United States to the end of the century.

ImpactThe findings, models and underlying data form part of the IPCC 6th Assessment Report, informing worldwide environmental policy since 2021.

The business challenge

Climate policy needs numbers it can defend. Quantifying how a changing climate will move building energy demand across an entire country means modelling at a scale, and a level of transparency, that proprietary tools rarely offer — and a policy body cannot cite what it cannot inspect.

The strategy and execution

Science and engineering advance fastest when models, data and methods are open and reproducible. Both models were therefore built to be transparent and highly scalable, and released openly, so the method could be examined rather than taken on trust.

The outcome and impact

I delivered two complementary forecasting models: HBLM-USA, data-driven, and DEG-USA, physics-based, built with deep neural networks and Bayesian statistics to analyse energy consumption across one million buildings spanning the entire United States. They supplied data that directly informed the 2021 IPCC 6th Assessment Report on Climate Change, backed by 80+ publications and 2,500+ citations from more than a decade of research at ETH Zürich and the ETH-Singapore Centre.
